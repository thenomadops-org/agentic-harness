A hedged line — "this *may* be caused by X" — was copied into a runbook as "this
is caused by X", and by the time it reached an agent prompt it was a confident
causal fact driving a fix. Nobody lied. Each copy dropped one qualifier.

**Why:** hedges are the first thing lost in summary, because they read as noise
and cost words. The claim gets stronger every time it is restated, and the
restatement is what the next person cites.

**How to apply:** when a claim matters, **read the primary source**, not the
summary of it. If a doc asserts a cause, find the evidence. If there is none, the
claim is a hypothesis and should be written as one.

When you find a strengthened claim, **fix the source, not just the copy in front
of you**. The copy you are reading is a symptom; the skill, runbook or template
it came from will keep producing more.

Carry hedges forward deliberately. "We think" and "we measured" are different
claims and the difference is the whole value.
